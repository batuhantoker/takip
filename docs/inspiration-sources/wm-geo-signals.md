# World Monitor: geo-located signal sources (slice A)

Source: World Monitor clone at commit 968eac6 (AGPL-3.0). Only facts are recorded here: endpoints, parameters, fields and methods. No code was copied. Checklist: the geo slice of World Monitor's `shared/source-attribution-manifest.json` (164 providers, 172 hosts). Every provider in it appears below, either in the main table, the out-of-slice table, or the keyed table.

## Implementation status (2026-10-12)

Built from the facts below and re-probed before each one. All of it is uncommitted on `ui/shell-reorg`. Sources this doc shares with `provenance.md` (NHC, GDACS volcanoes, gpsjam, UNHCR, FCDO, Statuspage, adsb.lol, TeleGeography) belong to that work; its status section covers them.

- **Weather and civil alerts → `/api/weather`** (Severe Weather layer, Live Alerts):
  - **WMO SWIC** (`weather/wmo-swic.ts`). `wmo_all.json` has no coordinates, so each alert's CAP file (`/v2/cap-alerts/{capURL}`) is read once for its polygon or circle. Kept: Severe and Extreme (`s` ≥ 3) outside the US and Canada, about 260 alerts. Members that publish region codes only (CMA, BoM, HNMS, Meteoam…) become one country-level event per member, not stacked pins.
  - **ECCC** (`weather/eccc.ts`): one event per alert area, `issued` and `continued` paged separately; severity from the risk colour.
  - **Hong Kong Observatory** `warnsum` (`weather/hko.ts`). HKO publishes nothing through SWIC.
  - **Canadian civil alerts** (`weather/canada-civil.ts`): B.C. evacuation orders and alerts, SaskAlert minus Environment Canada's duplicates, Alberta Emergency Alert (empty when built, so its parser follows GeoRSS/CAP).
  - **GDACS volcanoes** (the provenance work's `weather/gdacs-volcanoes.ts`) wired here; EONET volcanoes within 100 km of a GDACS one are dropped (`weather/dedupe.ts`).
  - The weather popup now escapes issuer text and shows the issuer and area.
- **Radiation → `/api/radiation`**, which revives the existing Radiation layer (its May 2026 route made readings up and was reverted):
  - **EPA RadNet**: 31 curated fixed monitors, each rated against its own previous 168 readings (WM's bands: +8/+15 nSv/h or z 2/3; σ floored at 1 nSv/h). A monitor silent for 6 h is OFFLINE, not dropped (NYC since 2026-07, Chicago since 2026-09). Denver publishes counts without a dose rate and is left out. The endpoint needs a non-JSON `Accept` (406 otherwise).
  - **Safecast**: `tt.safecast.org/devices` instead of `measurements.json`, about 150 real-time sensors reporting in the last day. Latest values only, so UNRATED.
- **War and air-raid alerts → `/api/war-alerts`** and the existing `war_alerts` slot (Air-Raid Alerts, THREAT):
  - **Tzeva Adom** `notifications` plus `alerts-history`, placed with Tzeva Adom's own `static/cities.json`. Threat ids from its client: 0 rockets, 1 hazmat, 2 infiltration, 3 earthquake, 4 tsunami, 5 UAV, 6 non-conventional, 7 radiological, 8 general, 9 drill.
  - **Ukraine**: `ubilling.net.ua/aerialalerts/`, not alerts.com.ua (see below). Oblast alerts are anchored at the administrative centre; a mirror more than 10 minutes behind is reported as down, never as all-clear.
- **Canadian wildfire incidents → `/api/fires`** (`fire_incidents`, drawn under Active Fires): CWFIS `cwfif_national_activefires` filtered to the versions current now (the layer keeps every past version, ~190k rows), joined to BC Wildfire by fire number for names, links and "fire of note".
- **FAA NAS Status → `/api/airport-status`** and a new Airport Delays layer (AVIATION). WM's delay bands; a closure limited to a class of aircraft ("CLSD TO NON SKED TRANSIENT GA") is minor and `partial`, since most last months.
- **IMF PortWatch → `/api/chokepoints`** and a new Chokepoint Traffic layer (MARITIME). WM's 7-day vs ~90-day method, anchored to the latest published day (weekly upstream), with a 2 vessels/day baseline floor. An active red PortWatch disruption outranks the ratio on the map: Hormuz's baseline is already the collapse.
- **Travel advisories → `/api/advisories`** (US State Department and Smartraveller, whole feeds; `?iso2=` adds the provenance work's FCDO) and the region dossier's `travel_advice`, all on FCDO's 0–4 scale (US 1/2/3/4 → 0/1/2/4; AU 2/5–5/5 → 0/1/2/4). **Smartraveller is unreachable from this network** (Akamai drops the whole host); its parser was built from the 2026-10-01 Wayback copy and reports `ok: false` until it is tried from the server.
- **Disease outbreaks → `/api/outbreaks`** and a new Disease Outbreaks layer (HAZARD): WHO DON (OData), CDC travel notices and ECDC epidemiological updates and risk assessments. One ring per country, never a pin. Kept: a known disease and a country, under 90 days except CDC (which lists a notice only while it is in force); the highest CDC level wins over a newer, lower notice. A notice naming more than 50 countries (CDC's Global Measles, 238) is listed under `worldwide` instead of drawn.
- **NGA MSI → `/api/nav-warnings`**, route only, no layer. The API has served nothing issued after **2024-05-10** (its own `latest-warning` says so, and NGA's NavWarnings page uses the same endpoint); the route reports `latestIssued` and `stale`.
- **Not built:**
  - **HDX HAPI**: both `hapi.humdata.org` and `data.humdata.org` answer 406 "Blocked due to bot activity" to this network, with or without an app identifier.
  - **alerts.com.ua**: live for Kyiv city only. On 2026-10-12 it showed 2 oblasts on alert while ubilling's mirror showed 16 during the same attack.
  - **C2IntelFeeds**: bare IPs with no hosting country; placing them means bulk IP geolocation.
  - **GCP `incidents.json`**: 7 incidents, none active; AWS health now serves HTML.
  - **CorridorRisk**: needs a forged Referer on an undocumented API.
  - **OREF** (Israeli IP only), **ThinkGlobalHealth** (bundle scrape), **USNI**, **PizzINT**, **Toronto** feeds, **TTC** (protobuf), **VIA Rail**, **WTO Hormuz** (Power BI scrape), climate series and news RSS: low value for the map or scrapes.

## Summary

- **Counts (164 providers, 172 hosts in the chunk):** 133 keyless, 23 free key or free registration, 7 paid, and 1 placeholder that is not a source. The 133 keyless include 31 cloud status pages, 16 news RSS feeds (9 aviation, 7 climate), 13 US-embassy alert feeds, about 8 scrapes, and 7 out-of-slice relay neighbours. That leaves about 55 keyless geo-signal sources.
- **Probes:** 42 sequential GETs. 39 returned 200. There were 3 failures, both of which matter: **UCDP now returns 401 without a token**, and **Ontario 511 and Alberta 511 now return 400 without a key**. That second one breaks two of OSIRIS's own `api/cctv` camera sub-sources.
- **Top 10 for OSIRIS** (keyless, located, live, and new to OSIRIS unless noted):
  1. **WMO SWIC `wmo_all.json` + ECCC OGC alerts.** Official CAP warnings worldwide plus Canadian polygons. OSIRIS's weather alerts are NWS-only today.
  2. **NGA MSI broadcast warnings.** Public domain, worldwide. Navigational hazards, live-fire areas and cable-repair notices; one JSON call.
  3. **IMF PortWatch ArcGIS layers.** Daily chokepoint transits, daily port calls by vessel type, and disruption events, all with lat/lon. They upgrade OSIRIS's static ports.
  4. **Tzeva Adom + pikud-haoref `cities.json`.** Real-time Israeli siren alerts with no proxy. Unofficial API, so a terms risk; OREF itself needs an Israeli egress IP.
  5. **FAA NAS Status XML.** Public-domain US ground stops and delays. Item 6 of DATA-SOURCES.md's "add these first" list.
  6. **NOAA NHC MapServer** cones, tracks and points, plus GDACS volcanoes via `SEARCH?eventlist=VO`. Public-domain hazard polygons.
  7. **EPA RadNet + Safecast.** A radiation layer from US fixed monitors and crowd sensors. WM's baseline and z-score method is described above.
  8. **CWFIS (NRCan) + BC Wildfire WFS.** Agency-reported fire incidents with size and stage under OGL, complementing FIRMS hotspots.
  9. **gpsjam.org daily H3 CSV.** The fastest route to a GPS-jamming layer. The licence is unclear, so plan to compute it ourselves from adsb.lol NIC/NACp later.
  10. **UNHCR population API + HDX HAPI** (self-made app identifier). Displacement flows by country pair and admin-level conflict aggregates, no key.
- **Honourable mentions:** a Canada emergency bundle (B.C. evacuation polygons, SaskAlert CAP, Alberta Emergency Alert, BC Open511 events); GCP `incidents.json` and AWS health for cloud-region outage points; Planespotters photos; the ThinkGlobalHealth outbreak points (the only outbreak data with coordinates, but it is a scrape).

## Answers to the lead's specific questions

- **GPS jamming:** WM gets it from gpsjam.org's public static files, with no key. `https://gpsjam.org/data/manifest.csv` has one row per day (`date,suspect,num_bad_hexes`), and the last row is the latest date. Then `https://gpsjam.org/data/{date}-h3_4.csv` has one row per H3 resolution-4 hex (`hex,count_good_aircraft,count_bad_aircraft`). WM drops hexes seen by fewer than 3 aircraft and computes bad / (good + bad): under 2% is low (hidden), 2–10% medium, over 10% high. It tags each hex to one of 12 bounding-box regions and maps hex centroids to countries for its risk score. Updated daily. In 2026-07, WM moved back to gpsjam.org from the Wingbits customer API after monthly quota exhaustion (HTTP 402) left the layer silently stale. gpsjam is derived from ADS-B Exchange and states no licence. For a commercial OSIRIS, computing it ourselves from the NIC/NACp fields in adsb.lol is the cleaner route; DATA-SOURCES.md already proposes that.
- **AIS relay upstream:** aisstream.io over WebSocket (`wss://stream.aisstream.io/v0/stream`). It needs a free key (`AISSTREAM_API_KEY`). WM subscribes to the whole globe (`BoundingBoxes [[[-90,-180],[90,180]]]`) with `FilterMessageTypes: PositionReport, ShipStaticData`. It caches ShipType and name from the type-5 static messages by MMSI, because position reports carry no ship type, so tankers (ship types 80–89) can't be classified without them. Everything is derived on WM's side: a 2° density grid with a 30-minute baseline and alerts at ±30%, dark-ship gaps over 60 minutes, chokepoint crossing counts, and naval identification from the flag-state digits of the MMSI plus a list of named ships. OSIRIS already uses aisstream (`api/maritime`).
- **Military flights without a paid feed:** the primary source is `https://api.adsb.lol/v2/mil`. It is keyless and ODbL, and returns all military-flagged aircraft worldwide in one call. WM then classifies them in four steps: (1) a table of military ICAO 24-bit address blocks by country, (2) callsign-prefix patterns (US airlift, tanker, VIP and ISR callsigns, and others), (3) hand-curated overrides for individual addresses (for example PLAAF ISR and tanker airframes), and (4) optional Wingbits enrichment that matches owner and operator names against keyword lists. Airplanes.live and adsb.fi point queries over 8 blind-spot regions are switched off by default because both are non-commercial; they are enabled only with `WM_ENABLE_NONCOMMERCIAL_ADSB_GAP_FILL=1`. OpenSky is fully disabled in the military seeder.
- **NOTAMs and airport delays:** US delays come from the FAA NAS Status XML (`nasstatus.faa.gov/api/airport-status-information`), which is keyless. It has Ground_Delay, Ground_Stop, Arrival/Departure delay and closure blocks, each with ARPT, Reason, and Avg or Min/Max values. NOTAMs come from the ICAO API Data Service (`dataservices.icao.int/api/notams-realtime-list?api_key=…&format=json&locations=ICAO,ICAO…`), which is keyed and covers 46 MENA airports. A NOTAM counts as a closure when its Q-code subject is FA/AH/AL/AW/AC/AM and its condition is LC/AS/AU/XX/AW, or when the text says "AD CLSD", "AIRPORT CLOSED" or "AIRSPACE CLOSED". Q-codes RA and RO mark restricted areas. International delays come from AviationStack (keyed). WM uses no keyless NOTAM source.
- **Rocket and air-raid alerts:** what the code does differs from the docs. The docs describe polling OREF through a proxy. In the code, the primary source is **Tzeva Adom** (`https://api.tzevaadom.co.il/notifications`): keyless and unofficial, returning a JSON array with `notificationId, threat, cities[], desc, date`. OREF (`www.oref.org.il/WarningMessages/alert/alerts.json` and `/History/AlertsHistory.json`) is only a fallback, used when `OREF_PROXY_AUTH` is set. Akamai blocks non-Israeli IPs and Node's TLS fingerprint, so WM fetches it with curl through an Israeli residential proxy, sending the headers `Referer: https://www.oref.org.il/` and `X-Requested-With: XMLHttpRequest`. Hebrew city names are translated and placed on the map using the `cities.json` file from github.com/eladnava/pikud-haoref-api. WM polls every 5 minutes, groups alerts into waves by timestamp, and sorts threats into missile, rocket, drone, mortar, infiltration, earthquake, tsunami and hazmat.
- **IMD and other cyclone feeds:** IMD RSMC New Delhi (`api.imd.gov.in`) needs an account, a key, an email and a password. WM mints a JWT through `POST /api/oauth/token.php` and calls from a static outbound IP. The API is free for non-commercial use only. The keyless cyclone sources WM uses are the NOAA NHC MapServer (Atlantic, East Pacific and Central Pacific: track, cone and points), GDACS TC, and the Hong Kong Observatory `warnsum` local warning signals. JMA (an experimental CAP feed, "not operational") and JTWC are both explicitly *blocked* in WM's code; it makes zero requests to them.
- **Radiation:** two networks. **EPA RadNet** fixed monitors (10 US cities) via `radnet.epa.gov/cdx-radnet-rest/api/rest/csv/{year}/fixed/{STATE}/{CITY}`, returning a CSV of approved gamma readings. **Safecast** via `api.safecast.org/measurements.json?distance=120&latitude=&longitude=&captured_after=` (km radius, with 400 days of lookback). WM converts CPM to µSv/h at 350 CPM per µSv/h. A reading is a spike when it is ≥15 above baseline or has z ≥ 3, and elevated at ≥8 or z ≥ 2. WM does not use EURDEP or any IAEA feed.
- **Disease outbreaks (WHO/ProMED-style):** WHO Disease Outbreak News comes from an OData JSON API (`www.who.int/api/emergencies/diseaseoutbreaknews?...`), because the DON RSS has been dead since 2024. Alongside it: CDC HAN RSS; two ECDC RSS feeds; the UN Geneva newsroom Atom feed, filtered to items whose title ends in " - WHO"; and 10 per-disease CIDRAP RSS feeds. **ProMED-style point data comes only from the ThinkGlobalHealth (CFR) disease tracker.** Its `index_bundle.js` embeds about 1,600 geo-tagged alerts (with lat/lng) sourced from ProMED. WM extracts them by parsing the bundle, which is fragile: the format broke in 2026-04. It is the only outbreak source with coordinates; the rest give country names only.
- **Cloud status pages:** 31 pages, listed in the status-page rows below: 18 Atlassian Statuspage, 4 incident.io, 2 status.io, 1 Instatus, and AWS, Azure, GCP, Slack and Stripe with their own formats. All are keyless.
- **Submarine cables and pipelines:** cables come from TeleGeography's `www.submarinecablemap.com/api/v3/cable/all.json` plus one `/api/v3/cable/{slug}.json` per cable for roughly 50 strategic cables. It is keyless, but the data is proprietary. Cable *health* comes from NGA MSI navigational warnings (`msi.nga.mil/api/publications/broadcast-warn?output=json&status=A`), scanned for cable fault and repair-ship text. Pipelines are a one-off import of Global Energy Monitor's Global Oil & Gas Infrastructure Trackers (CC BY 4.0, download behind registration). An operator converts the files and commits them to `scripts/data/pipelines-{gas,oil}.json`; nothing is fetched at runtime.
- **UCDP, HAPI, UNHCR and DTM:**
  - **UCDP** GED is at `ucdpapi.pcr.uu.se/api/gedevents/{version}?pagesize=1000&page=N`. Versions are annual `YY.1` releases and monthly candidates `YY.0.M`. WM sends the header `x-ucdp-access-token` from `UCDP_ACCESS_TOKEN` and treats it as optional in code. **Probe: an unauthenticated `gedevents/25.1?pagesize=1&page=0` returned HTTP 401, so UCDP now requires a token.** Tokens are free on request from UCDP (by email). Fetch the monthly candidate releases (`YY.0.M`), whose lag is about one month, on top of the annual `YY.1` release.
  - **HDX HAPI** is at `hapi.humdata.org/api/v2/coordination-context/conflict-events?output_format=json&admin_level={0,2}&start_date=&limit=10000&offset=&location_code=ISO3`. It needs no key, only the header `X-HDX-HAPI-APP-IDENTIFIER`, set to base64 of `appname:email`, which you make up yourself. HAPI now bot-blocks WM's cloud egress. WM's primary channel is therefore the HDX CKAN dataset `hdx-hapi-conflict-event` (an annual CSV of about 19 MB, found via `data.humdata.org/api/3/action/package_show`), with the API as fallback.
  - **UNHCR** is keyless: `api.unhcr.org/population/v1/population/?year=&limit=10000&page=&coo_all=true&coa_all=true`.
  - **IOM DTM v3** (`dtmapi.iom.int/v3/displacement/...`) needs a free subscription key sent as `Ocp-Apim-Subscription-Key`.
- **Canadian 511 (actionable for OSIRIS):** WM's seeder header says Ontario 511 has rejected unkeyed requests with HTTP 400 "Invalid Key" since 2026-09-24, and Alberta 511 since 2026-08-19; Manitoba has always needed a key. OSIRIS's `api/cctv` route calls `511on.ca` and `511.alberta.ca`. **The probe confirms it:** unkeyed `511on.ca/api/v2/get/event?format=json` and `511.alberta.ca/api/v2/get/event?format=json` both returned HTTP 400 (a 45-byte XML error). OSIRIS calls `/api/v2/get/cameras` on both hosts with no key (`src/app/api/cctv/route.ts` lines 179 and 181), so those two camera sub-sources are very likely failing today. Request developer keys or drop them. OSIRIS's other 511 hosts use the same `/api/v2/get/` vendor pattern (511ga.org, 511in.org, 511la.org, 511pa.com, 511wi.gov, az511.gov, 511.idaho.gov, 511.alaska.gov) and may follow; they were not probed.
