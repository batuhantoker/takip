# Public pages and search

How TAKIP shows up in search results and link previews. Built 2026-10-19. The map itself is WebGL, which crawlers and link-preview bots can't read, so everything they see is server-rendered around it.

## Search metadata

What every public page gives a search engine, decided 2026-10-21. A page that can't meet it stays `noindex, follow`.

- **Title**, 60 characters at most (32 in Chinese and Japanese), the words people type first and the brand last (the layout's ` · TAKIP`): the place with its country or state, then the topic in its searched words ("Fleurus, Belgium webcams and live cameras: 6 on a map", "Paris, France weather: forecast, radar and live map"). The home page too, since nobody searches for the name yet.
- **Description**, 110 to 160 characters (40 to 95 in Chinese and Japanese), saying what this page holds that others don't: counts, sources, the forecast's source, the place. An event leads with its Wikipedia article's opening sentences in the page's language, the definition readers want, cut at a sentence (`leadSentences`); without one, its kind, date and place, never its name twice. No sentence that reads the same on every page of a kind.
- **One URL per language**, with every language's alternate, and the copy in that language's own search words ("hava durumu", "caméras en direct"), not a literal translation.
- **Structured data** only where it is true: breadcrumbs everywhere, `Event` on events (no offers, organizer or performer for history; Search Console's warnings for those stay), `ImageObject` for the live images. Search Console's optional-field warnings are not errors; "Validate fix" on one that can't be filled always fails.
- **Weight**: the HTML carries the page, not the app. The UI catalog loads as its own cached file (`locale-provider.tsx`), so a page in another language weighs what the English one does.

## Search scope

On 2026-10-29, between 17:00 and 18:00 UTC, Google demoted the whole site at once. Impressions fell from ~300 an hour to ~3 across every page kind and language, in web and image search alike, and the queries left sat at positions 60–90 instead of ~13. Search Console showed no manual action, no security issue and no removals. Pages stayed "Submitted and indexed" and no deploy landed that day. At that point Google knew ~1.15M URLs: 1.116M were "Discovered – currently not indexed" and 9,239 had ever been shown. The camera pages and `/map` pages earned the clicks; sun, ISS, Ramadan years, compare and event pages took impressions and almost none. On 2026-11-01 the user chose to cut what search is offered to the pages with data of their own (`lib/platform/site/search-scope.ts`):

- **Place topics**: cameras, transit and earthquakes (`hazards`), each still behind its own bar. Overviews: countries as before, and cities that meet the two-topic bar and have one of those pages. Other topics, counties, states and towns render `noindex, follow`.
- **Languages**: only `/map` and its layer pages are searched in every language and name alternates. Everything else is searched in English only, and its other languages render `noindex` with links to them `nofollow`.
- **Out entirely**: `/compare`, `/event`, `/mountain`, `/mountains`, and `/county`, `/state` and `/town`, whose only searched pages were plate readers. Their sitemaps list nothing. Space pages (fixed rosters, hand-written) stay in, in English.
- **Result**: about 15,000 place URLs, the `/map` pages in ten languages and the English space, date and policy pages, down from ~2.3M sitemap entries.

`readingMetadata` applies the language and family rules, `placeIndexed`/`searched` the topic rules, and the sitemaps and `crawlRel` links follow both. Recovery from a site-wide demotion takes weeks to months of recrawling, and there is nothing to file a reconsideration request against. Widen one topic or family at a time, once Search Console shows the kept pages ranking again.

## The address

Canonical links, the sitemap and preview images need the site's public origin. `SITE_URL` sets it: `https://takip.dev` on the NUC. Where it is unset (dev, self-hosted copies) each request's own host is used (`lib/platform/site/origin.ts`). While it is unset every page renders per request. TI's watch links use the same helper after `AI_PUBLIC_ORIGIN`.

## Adding or changing a layer page

- Add the entry to the section's file in `lib/platform/site/catalog/`. Everything in it is shown as written, so it has to be true of the code: real sources, real refresh rates, real limits. Estimates, inferences and timetables are called what they are.
- Titles stay within 60 characters and descriptions between 110 and 160 (`layer-pages.test.ts`).
- A layer added to the Layers panel needs a page or a line in `NO_PAGE` with the reason (`components/panels/layers/lib/layer-pages-coverage.test.ts`).
- A page whose layer needs a key (`requiresEnv`) exists only when the server has it, so no page promises a hidden layer. Today that is ships (`AIS_API_KEY`), the Cloudflare layers and road traffic (`TOMTOM_API_KEY`; without it traffic covers only the Netherlands, Rennes and Nantes).
- `near` opens the live map and "Open on the map" around the visitor at that zoom (2026-10-21): local layers zoom in (rain radar 6, flights 6, cameras 6, transit 7), worldwide ones take their own zoom and only turn the globe. Leave it out where the view is the subject (a hurricane basin, chokepoints, GPS jamming, air-raid alerts, US-only feeds, History). The picture and a spot's link keep the fixed view.
- `layers` replaces the map's whole set when the link opens (`use-startup.ts`), so `mapHref` adds the quiet display layers (day and night, the sky, the SDK lines) to any it names. A page that names none (History, Space, the tools) leaves the visitor's own layers alone.

## Layout

Laid out as the place pages are (`docs/design/README.md`, Reading pages; 2026-10-26). A layer page's tabs are its section's other pages, sticky under the breadcrumb (`PageTabs`), and the trail names the section (`/map#air`). The heading, lede, map button and live line head one column; on pages with an embed the tall live map stays beside it on a wide screen, and the places to start are rows that mark the map and fly it on a click (`data-map`, `data-map-jump`). Then what you see, the latest image, the places of its topic, the section's next page as a picture card ("Up next") and the pages that go with it, all hairline rows (`LayerRows`), and a closing band with what it can't tell you. Its sources and their freshness are one line in the footer, under the site's GeoNames credit (`SiteFrame`'s `credits`). The footer of every reading page folds its language links under the current language's name (2026-10-26: few readers switch; the head's hreflang alternates carry the rest). A page without an embed (another view, a panel) leads with its picture beside the heading instead. `/map` has the sections as anchor tabs that mark the one being read, the live flight map in its header beside every live line (`RightNow`, streamed in Suspense), a filter that hides the rows a search leaves out while every link stays in the HTML (`CatalogFilter`, matching from a word's start, accents folded), and per section its first page as a picture card, kept in view beside the rest as rows. In space and Back in time split their rows under headings by catalog file (`SECTION_GROUPS`).

## Pictures

On pages with an embed (`hasEmbed`: layers on today's map at a view), the picture turns into the live map: on a wide screen once it scrolls into view, on a phone only when the reader taps "Explore it here", since it loads the whole map's code (`LiveWindow`). Until it has loaded, the picture links into the full map.

Each page has a picture of the map: its own "Open on the map" link, taken in headless Chrome with the interface hidden, so it reads the same in every language (`MapWindow`; the whole picture links into the live map). `nice npx tsx tools/site/shots.ts` takes them against the running app (`--only slug,slug`, `--force` to retake, about 25 minutes for all), answering the map-time and usage calls itself so the run spends no one's minutes and leaves nothing in the analytics. They go to `.cache/site-shots/` (`SITE_SHOTS_DIR`), outside git, so a server needs its own run or a copy. A page without one shows a sketch of the world with its places marked. Retake a page's picture when its layer changes how it looks.

Each page has one ad slot (`AD_SLOTS.page`), beside the text and never over the picture; it shows only where AdSense is configured and the plan has ads. AdSense's Auto ads are on as well (since 2026-10-19), so Google adds its own placements on every page that loads the script, the map included; `/embed` never loads it, in any language.

## Languages

Each reading page has a URL per language: English at `/map/earthquakes`, the others under a prefix, `/tr/map/earthquakes`. `src/proxy.ts` rewrites `/tr/…` to the one set of routes and passes the language in a header that `requestLocale()` puts before the cookie and the country (`lib/core/i18n/request.ts`); the unprefixed pages are always English, `/en/…` redirects there, and a bare `/tr` opens `/tr/map`. A page searched in every language (only `/map` since 2026-11-01, Search scope) names every language's URL as an alternate (hreflang, `x-default` English); the others and a page kept out of search (`unindexed` in `meta.ts`) name none, and links to a page or language kept out are `nofollow` (`crawlRel`); `sitemap.xml` does too, but the place and event sitemaps leave them to the pages, which keeps an entry a tenth of the size. Links between reading pages keep the reader's language (`localePath`); the map itself has one URL.

Short interface text goes through the shared translator (`t()`, catalogs in `lib/core/i18n/catalogs`). The long page copy is kept per page in `lib/platform/site/translations/<locale>.ts` (`copy.ts`), because with the English sentence as the key, editing one word would lose every translation. Anything missing stays English, and a list whose length no longer matches the English falls back to English. All nine languages were machine-translated on 2026-10-19 and cover every page; `translations.test.ts` fails if a page, a section or an About key is missing, or a list's length no longer matches. When you change English copy, change the same field in each language, or delete it there so English shows until it is translated. Generic source names ("Road authorities", "USGS and EMSC") translate through `sourceNames`; names of organisations stay as they are. 

## History's topics

Thirty-one `/map/` pages, one per kind of History event, in `catalog/past-conflict.ts` (battles, wars, military operations, genocides and massacres, terrorist attacks, nuclear explosions, missile launches), `past-politics.ts` (revolutions, coups, assassinations, treaties, peace agreements, riots, protests and strikes, murders and shootings, the transatlantic slave trade, border changes, World Heritage in danger), `past-nature.ts` (earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, tsunamis, tropical cyclones, fireballs, natural disasters since 1960 from GDIS, famines, plague and epidemics) and `past-accidents.ts` (fires, plane crashes, rail accidents, other accidents, ancient shipwrecks, rocket launches). Each page's copy comes from its own records: counts, spans, the sources and the shares that make its limits (where the events cluster, how many are placed only at an area, what the source leaves out), measured on `history.db` on 2026-10-20; a count is written "more than" or "about" so an import that adds to it leaves it true, but a new source for a kind needs its page's sources and limits revised. `eventKinds` names the kinds a page covers: their event pages link back to it, and it lists the largest of them by the family's measure (deaths, magnitude, yield, places hit, people embarked) and the first recorded in each century or decade, an indexed event first (`events/topic-events.ts`). "Open on the map" opens History on the family (`families=`) and the page's own kinds (`kinds=`), and plays a span (`from`, `to`). The History panel then says "Only: …" above the family switches, with × to draw every kind again; switching on a family outside those kinds also drops the filter.

## Event pages

`/event/<slug>`, one per event in `history.db`, every language at its own URL as the reading pages are: the event's facts as History's popup gives them (`lib/history/popup.ts`: the date at its precision, BCE included, the toll and its range, how it was placed), its place on a sketch of the world, the Wikipedia summary in the page's language where the event has an article (the importer's `attrs.wiki`, else the article's own Wikipedia, `events/article.ts`), every source that records it (the event and the duplicates merged into it, each with its licence and its own page for the record; past three, folded), the war it was part of, what it includes, related events, the indexed events nearby in time and place, and "See 480 BCE on the map" (History at its year and place, its family on; "Open on the map" before 3000 BCE, where History stops). The first screen says what the event was: under the date, place and toll, the article's opening sentences with their credit, else the record's own summary (GTD's), then the war it was part of and the map link. JSON-LD `Event` and a breadcrumb; a share card at `/og?event=<slug>`. History's popups link to `/event/<id>`, which answers with a 307 to the slug (ids change when `history.db` is rebuilt).

- **Headings**: a title longer than 70 characters (32 in Chinese and Japanese), a year page's line, is headed by its clauses that fit (`eventHeading`, also on the share card), and the page shows the whole line as its summary; the popup puts it behind Read more. Fact rows on the page are never cut.
- **Slugs** (`events/slug.ts`): the title folded to ASCII, then the start year, `battle-of-salamis-480-bce`; CE years are bare, and the year is left off when the title starts or ends with it (`1999-izmit-earthquake`). A title with no Latin letters gives the kind (`battle-1812`), so no slug is a bare number. When two events share a base, an indexed event gets it before others, then a canonical one, then the older record, and the others add six hex characters of a hash of their source code and record id (`gtd:201601010001`). A slug once given stays with its event (`tools/site/events/plan.ts`): the build reads the previous index, keeps each event's slug while its title and year give the same base, keeps an old slug as a 308 to the new one when they change, and never gives a slug to another event, even after its event is removed. A duplicate's slug 308s to its canonical event's page.
- **The index** is `data/db/event-pages.db`, beside `history.db`, which it must match: every event's key, slug, page facts for lists (title, kind, date, place, measure), whether it is indexed, every slug ever given, the sources and their licences, and an R*Tree of indexed events for "nearby". Rebuild it after every import with `nice npx tsx tools/site/build-event-pages.ts` (about 5 s and 1 GB of memory for 435,000 events; it writes a new file, about 210 MB, and renames it over the old). It is not in git: copy it to a server with `history.db` (`rsync -a data/db/event-pages.db user@nuc:takip/data/db/`). Where it is missing the event routes answer 404 and the sitemaps list none.
- **Reads**: the web server opens `event-pages.db` and `history.db` read-only (better-sqlite3, `events/store.ts`) and notices a replaced file within a minute. Every read is a key lookup or an R*Tree box (a few milliseconds for a war with 900 battles); history.db is looked up by the event's source and record id where the index is older than it. Pages are held per slug for 30 minutes (2,000 of them), Wikipedia summaries for a day (5,000); a summary slower than 2.5 s is left out of that render and shows on the next. Nothing reads `history.db` whole, and the routes are rendered when asked (the build machine has no `data/db`).
- **Indexing**: an event is indexed when it has a Wikipedia article of its own or two sources. An article several events carry (a volcano's for each of its eruptions, an earthquake's for the tsunami it raised, a territory's for each claim to it) counts for one of them: the one with more sources, then the larger measure, then the later. On 2026-10-20: 434,805 pages, 50,173 indexed, the rest `noindex, follow`: GTD's attacks, GCAT's launches and missiles, the slave voyages, IBTrACS's storms, PA-X's agreements and the other sets with no articles index only where two sources meet.
- **Sitemaps**: none since 2026-11-01 (Search scope); before, `/event/sitemap/<n>.xml`, indexed pages in every language, 5,000 events (50,000 entries, the limit) a file, numbered by date in the index so a file is one range read; 11 files. `sitemap-index.xml` and `robots.txt` list them.

## Space pages

Built 2026-10-24, after a search for "mars curiosity site:takip.dev" found nothing: the craft lived only in the WebGL space view.

- **The roster** (`space/roster.ts`) is fixed, so slugs never move: every craft with a mission note in `lib/space/craft/mission-notes.ts` (but Chandrayaan-3's propulsion module, which is part of its page), every landing site on the Moon and Mars, and Cassini. Slugs are the searched words (`curiosity-rover`, `voyager-1`, `apollo-11`) and each has an English Wikipedia title checked against the API. `roster.test.ts` fails when a new mission note or landing site has no page. `/spacecraft/-76` (a Horizons ID) sends a 307 to its page.
- **A craft page** says what it is (the mission note, else Wikipedia's opening line when the note is short), where it is now (the fleet route: region, distance and signal time from Earth, distance from the Sun and speed; for a craft in Earth orbit its height; for Curiosity and Perseverance NASA's sol, place and odometry from the surface route), its flight as JPL's trajectory has it (launch, flybys with their distance, arrivals, landings, the Voyagers' heliopause; steps after the trajectory was fetched are marked expected), launch date, rocket and pad, the landing site, NASA's 3D model credit, and Wikipedia's lead and picture in the page's language. Every "now" carries its moment and is left out when its route fails.
- **A planet page** answers how far it is from Earth now (the sky route: distance, light time, whether it is closing in), lists the craft JPL places there now (landers go with the landing sites), the craft whose fetched flights flew past, arrived or landed, and on Mars the rovers and every landing site.
- **Flights** are read from the files the history route keeps (`lib/space/replay/flight-files.ts`), never fetched from JPL for a page, so a craft nobody has replayed has no timeline; only the steps are held in memory, parsed back from the English labels `history/collect.ts` writes (`space/flights.ts`).
- **"Open in 3D"** opens the space view on the thing itself: `?view=space&focus=mars&rover=curiosity`, `&site=moon:apollo-11`, `&craft=-31`, `&body=jupiter`, and "Watch the flight" `&replay=-31` (`lib/map/view-mode-link.ts`, sent through `requestWorld`).
- **Indexing**: every page is indexed and in the sitemap, in English only since 2026-11-01 (Search scope); titles follow the search ("Where is Voyager 1 now? Live distance from Earth", "How far is Mars from Earth right now?"). Share cards are `/og?space=craft:<slug>`, `planet:<key>` or `index:`, fixed words over the space view's picture. The Space `/map` pages list them (`spaceLinks` in the catalog). The Moon and the Sun keep their `/map` pages.
- **Deep-sky pages** (2026-10-26), `/deep-sky/<id>` and the `/deep-sky` index: one per telescope picture the space view hangs (`lib/space/deep/deep-sky.ts`), slugged by the picture's id, which never moves (`pillars-of-creation`, `m31`). Each leads with the picture filling its frame, then the distance from the Sun, "Open in 3D" (`&feature=<id>`), what it is, who took the picture (Webb's and Hubble's link their craft pages), how wide it spans, its credit and Wikipedia's lead, and the others in its realm. Titles follow the search ("Pillars of Creation: where it is and how far, in 3D"); descriptions lead with the object's first sentence. Share cards are `/og?space=sky:<id>` (`sky:` for the index). The deep-sky cards on the Space `/map` pages lead to these pages, with a 3D shortcut on the picture's corner (`space/sky-pages.ts`, `components/site/space/sky-*.tsx`). Past the pictures the same route serves the large-scale universe (2026-10-27): Laniakea and its five neighbouring basins, the 13 named clusters, superclusters and walls, the voids (Boötes, the Local Void, the Sculptor Void's basin and Pustilnik's 25 nearest) and the microwave background, 48 pages. Each is a feature of the space view with a stable id (`laniakea`, `coma-cluster`, `hercules-void`), so its "Open in 3D" link and its page never drift apart; the page leads with Wikipedia's picture where the structure has an article (the Planck map for the background, the space view's own picture otherwise), the distance (a basin's attractor's), the space view card's note and facts, and Wikipedia's lead, and the index lists them under the pictures as text cards (`space/structure-pages.ts`, `components/site/space/structure-*.tsx`). The dwarf planets' and visitors' pages at `/planet/<key>` work out where each is now the way the space view does, from its outer-body feed and Earth's place in the sky one (`space/outer-page.ts`, `components/site/space/outer-*.tsx`); their 3D link is `&outer=<key>`.

## Mountain pages

Built 2026-10-26. Every mountain, hill and volcano on Wikidata (CC0) with coordinates on Earth and an article in a Wikipedia not written by a bot gets a page, about 78,000 of them.

- **The index** is `data/db/mountains.db`, built by `nice npx tsx tools/site/build-mountains.ts [--offline]` (`tools/site/mountains/`). Which items are mountains (P31 under mountain, hill, summit, massif or volcano, without ranges, seamounts, guyots, submarine volcanoes or other worlds' mons), their articles and their coordinates come from QLever over the whole class set, one property a query. Everything else comes from query.wikidata.org for those with an article, 500 items a query: height, prominence and isolation with their rank and unit (the preferred statement, else the median of the normal ones, feet converted), parent peak, range, countries, Commons picture, the Smithsonian volcano number, the first ascent (P793 with its P585 date) and the places it is the highest point of (P610, read from the other end). Then labels in the ten languages and the kinds of the places named. The bot-made Wikipedias left out are ceb, sv, war, lld, arz and ace (`BOT_WIKIS`). Every answer is cached in `.cache/site/mountains`. Each mountain also gets the nearest gazetteer city or town within about 300 km and its time zone (`topics/iss.json`), for the forecast's clock and the towns list. The file is not in git and is built from Wikidata alone, so copy it to a server as it is (`rsync -a data/db/mountains.db user@nuc:takip/data/db/`). Where it is missing, the routes answer 404 and the sitemaps list none.
- **Slugs** are the English name folded as event slugs are (`mont-blanc`, `k2`), else another Latin name, else the kind and the Wikidata number. When two share a name, the better known gets it bare. The next gets its English article's title (`mount-olympus-washington`), then the name and its country, then the name and its number. A slug once given stays with its mountain even if Wikidata renames it, and is never given to another. A slug whose mountain was merged into another 308s to it.
- **A page** leads with the height (in feet first for a US peak), Wikipedia's opening in the page's language and its picture (else the Commons picture), what it is the highest point of, and "See it in 3D": the map at the summit at zoom 12.5 with 3D terrain and hiking routes on, tilted. Then come MET Norway's forecast at the summit's own height (`/api/forecast?altitude=`: on Everest the model's ground is 8,269 m and 3.5 °C warmer), with its first hour in the header labelled as a forecast, and the facts from Wikidata. After those come the peaks within 60 km, the parent peak and the peaks below it, the highest in its range and in its country with this one in its place, and webcams within 30 km. A volcano lists its eruptions from History (`hazard.volcano` events filed under its English article) and every page lists History's indexed events within 20 km. Last are the towns within 80 km, linking their place pages. Every row marks the live map beside the list (`data-map`, `map-talk.ts`). JSON-LD is `Mountain` (and `Volcano`) with `geo.elevation`, plus the breadcrumb.
- **Indexing**: out of search since 2026-11-01 (Search scope). Before that, a page was indexed when its height is known and it has an English article or articles in two of the page languages; the rest render `noindex, follow` and stay out of `/mountain/sitemap/<n>.xml` (every language, 50,000 entries a file, in the index's order of how many Wikipedias have an article). Titles put the name and country first, then the words searched after a mountain's name: "Mount Ararat, Turkey: height, weather and map". A volcano with recorded eruptions gets "Mount Etna volcano, Italy: height, eruptions and map". Each language has its own searched words ("yüksekliği, hava durumu", "altura, clima y mapa"), not yet checked against autocomplete.
- **Lists** rank separate mountains only: a summit with under 300 m of prominence, or without a known prominence and with a higher peak within about 2 km, counts as part of the higher one (Everest's South Summit, Lhotse Middle). `/mountains` has the 50 highest, each continent's highest point (P610 on a continent) and every country's list. `/mountains/<country>` (the country page's slug, `/mountains/turkey`) has its 100 highest, indexed where five of its peaks are. Both go in `sitemap.xml`. Share cards are `/og?mountain=<slug>` and `/og?mountains=world` or `=TR`.

## Ramadan dates

`/ramadan` (every language at `/tr/ramadan` and so on; `app/ramadan`, `components/site/ramadan/ramadan-dates.tsx`) answers when Ramadan begins and ends. It is worked out when the page renders, from ICU's Umm al-Qura calendar, so it rolls on with the clock and stores nothing (`lib/platform/site/ramadan-dates.ts` on `places/topics/ramadan-calendar.ts`): the Ramadan under way or next and the one after, this and next Gregorian year's with first tarawih, Laylat al-Qadr (the night before the 27th) and both Eids, and a table from ten years back to ten ahead, 2030 with its two Ramadans. Its title and description carry the coming Ramadan's year and dates, which only the dates pages may do: a place topic's can't depend on the day. It links the city Ramadan timetables of sixteen cities, and they and the prayer-times pages link back. JSON-LD is the breadcrumb only.

## Easter dates

`/easter` (`app/easter`, `components/site/dates/easter-dates.tsx`) is built the same way, worked out when the page renders (`lib/platform/site/easter-dates.ts`): Western Easter by the Gregorian computus (the anonymous algorithm of Meeus, Jones and Butcher), Orthodox Easter by Meeus's Julian algorithm, turned into its Gregorian date through the Julian day number, so the 13-day gap becomes 14 after 2100 on its own. For this and next Gregorian year it gives Ash Wednesday, Palm Sunday, Good Friday, Easter Monday, Ascension and Pentecost, the Orthodox Good Friday, Easter and Pentecost, and Christmas on 25 December and on the Julian calendar (7 January of the same Gregorian year); the table runs from ten years back to ten ahead and marks the years both Easters fall together. The lead sentence, title and description follow this year's Easter until its Orthodox Easter has passed, then next year's. The tests hold the computus to the WCC's table of both Easters for 2016–2036 (Wikipedia's Template:Table of dates of Easter).

## Jewish holidays

`/jewish-holidays` (`app/jewish-holidays`, `components/site/dates/jewish-holidays.tsx`, `lib/platform/site/jewish-holidays.ts`) does the same from ICU's Hebrew calendar. It finds 1 Tishri and each month's first day from the day and year ICU gives, not its English month names, so a leap year's Adar II needs no special case, and reads off each Gregorian year's Purim, Passover (eight days, seven in Israel), Shavuot (two days, one in Israel), Tisha B'Av (put off to Sunday when the 9th of Av is Shabbat), Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Simchat Torah (the 23rd of Tishri, the 22nd in Israel) and Hanukkah. A date is a holiday's first full day, and the copy says it begins at sunset the evening before. The lead sentence names the Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur of the year whose Yom Kippur is still to begin, and the next Passover; the title takes that Rosh Hashanah's year; the table marks the holiday under way or next. All 21 years of the table (2016–2036) agree with hebcal.com's API, Israel's days included; the tests keep a sample. It lists the indexed Shabbat-times pages by country, Israel first (`shabbatCities()`), since city pages don't link them (`faith-reach.ts`).

The three dates pages link each other in one line (`components/site/dates/other-dates.tsx`). Like `/ramadan`, the two new pages carry their year and dates in the title and description, and their JSON-LD is the breadcrumb only.

## Live lines

A page with `live` fetches one sentence when it renders. It goes over loopback to the app's own `/api` route (`live/own-api.ts`, `WEB_INTERNAL_URL` or `127.0.0.1:$PORT`) rather than importing the route, so there is one copy of each route's cache and no extra upstream calls. A failure leaves the line out. The line says the time it was true for ("as of 14:11 UTC"), never "live".
