# Place pages

Public pages about a place and a topic: `/city/cincinnati/transit`, `/city/nevsehir/cameras`, `/country/turkey/…`, and in the US `/state/georgia/…`, `/county/fulton-county-georgia/…` and `/town/beverly-hills-california/…`, each in every language (`/tr/city/nevsehir/cameras`). They bring search traffic for what people actually type ("Cincinnati bus tracker", "nevşehir kameraları izle"), so each one has to be about that place, from real data. Built 2026-10-19 on top of the layer pages (`public-pages.md`), whose frame, map window and language handling they reuse.

## Pieces

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| Places: every town of 15,000 people or more and every country, keyed by GeoNames id, with names in our languages from Wikidata | `data/site/gazetteer.json`, built by `tools/site/build-gazetteer.ts`; read by `lib/platform/site/places/gazetteer.ts` |
| US states, counties and towns (incorporated places the GeoNames list leaves out, with something to show), each US city's county and the Census place that is the city | `data/site/us-areas.json`, built by `tools/site/build-us-areas.ts` from the `us-counties`, `us-places` and `alpr` layers in `data/db`; merged into the gazetteer, ids in `places/us-areas.ts` |
| A topic's data, keyed by GeoNames id | `data/site/topics/<topic>.json`, built by `tools/site/topics/<topic>.ts`; read with `topicTable()` (`places/tables.ts`) |
| A topic: which pages a place has, their copy, where "Open the live map" lands, what is live | `lib/platform/site/places/topics/<topic>.ts`, listed in `topics/registry.ts` |
| How a topic's page renders | `components/site/places/topics/<topic>-body.tsx`, listed in `components/site/places/topics/registry.ts` |
| One page for every place and topic | `components/site/places/place-page.tsx`, behind `app/city/[slug]/[[...topic]]` and `app/country/[slug]/[[...topic]]` |
| Indexes | `/city` (the biggest places, and countries), `/country`, and `/state`, `/county` and `/town` (by state, `areas-index.tsx`); an overview lists the places inside: a country's states and cities, a state's counties and cities, a county's cities and towns |

## Adding a topic

1. A builder in `tools/site/topics/<topic>.ts` that writes `data/site/topics/<topic>.json` (`TopicTable`: `generated`, optional `meta`, `rows` by GeoNames id). Only real data: no row where there is nothing to say. Cameras are the exception: they are counted from the app's own camera catalogue when the page renders (`topics/camera-catalogue.ts`).
2. `lib/platform/site/places/topics/<topic>.ts` exporting a `PlaceTopic` (`places/topic.ts`). `topics/transit.ts` is the reference.
   - `pages(place)` returns the topic's pages for a place: usually one (`slug: 'transit'`), several for ranges (`earthquakes-last-10-years`, `-last-100-years`). It must be cheap and offline, a table lookup: the sitemap and the "nearby" cards call it for thousands of places.
   - `index` on each page says whether it is worth a search result. Below the bar the page still renders, with `noindex, follow`, and stays out of the sitemap and the cards; it names no other languages, and links to it are `nofollow` (`placeIndexed`), so crawlers spend their visits on pages that can rank. Agreed bars: transit, one operator with live positions; cameras, two on air; flights, an airport within 50 km and 100,000 people; weather, a METAR station plus radar or lightning, and 50,000 people; ports, a medium or large harbour credited to the place within 10 km, one of the map's major ports, or a small harbour where AIS heard 50 ships, and in each case at least 10 ships heard there in the AIS sample; ISS, 100,000 people where the station can climb 10° or more (within about 64.5° of the equator); sunrise and sunset, 50,000 people or anywhere the midnight sun reaches (65.7° and beyond), dropping a place within 25 km of a more populous indexed one and the smaller of two places named alike in one region; prayer times and Ramadan, the same bar (`sunIndexed`) where `faith-reach.ts` gives them pages, Ramadan only for the next four; Shabbat times, the same bar or a city in `jewish-communities.json`; aurora, 55° of magnetic latitude in the north and 51.5° in the south, where Tasmania and southern New Zealand watch the southern lights. ISS and aurora also drop a place within 25 km (ISS) or 50 km (aurora) of a more populous indexed one, since its numbers would be the same (`topics/sky-rules.ts`). Plate readers, 10 readers for a city, 5 for a town or county, 1 for a state or country (`ALPR_INDEX`). Earthquakes, five distinct events in the range: USGS quakes at its floor, NOAA's significant quakes USGS doesn't list at that floor, eruptions, and tsunamis no counted event caused (`hazardIndexed` in `topics/hazards-facts.ts`). A city gets earthquake pages only with 100,000 people, five NOAA-listed events within 100 km, and no bigger such city within 25 km, and keeps only its indexed ranges. Statistics (a country's History indicators), a measure with 10 yearly points, the latest within 15 years of the build, and not one value throughout; the hub from five indexed measures (`INDICATOR_INDEX`, `HUB_INDEX` in `topics/indicators-facts.ts`). The bar counts the page's own series, so the modelled climate pages (days above 35 °C, tropical nights) pass on the CMIP6 historical run and say "modelled" throughout; a country where the model gives no hot days at all stays unindexed.
   - `header()` gives the title (60 characters at most), description (110 to 160), heading and lede, through `t()` so they can be translated; name the parameter `t`, because the catalog extractor only sees `t(…)` and `msg(…)`. Use `fit()` to fall back to shorter wordings, and wrap a title's wordings in `placeFirst()`, which tries each with the place's country or state ("Fleurus, Belgium webcams and live cameras", the way people also search) before the name alone. `topics/topics.test.ts` checks every indexed page.
   - `live()` is where network calls go: over loopback to the app's own routes with `ownApi`, never to an upstream. Its answer fills the header's "right now" box, on the overview too. `detail()` is the same for what only the topic's own page shows below the header (weather's forecast); its answer reaches the view's `Body` and `Wide` as `detail`.
   - A topic with many pages can keep all but one out of the place's nav and overview with `listed: false` on the others: the listed one stands for them and is marked current on them (statistics: the hub is listed, its 39 measures are not; earthquakes: the whole record is listed as "Earthquakes", the 10- and 100-year ranges are not, and each range's page switches between them). A country's nav leads with public holidays, statistics and earthquakes, plate readers last, and its overview opens on live flights and public cameras with a "Live in" block linking its biggest cities' indexed camera, weather, transit and flight pages (`places/country-live.ts`). `openLabel` renames the header's map button where other words say better what it does ("Watch it change on the map", "See them on the live map").
3. `components/site/places/topics/<topic>-body.tsx` exporting a `TopicView` (icon, `Body`, optional `Live`, and an optional `Hero` the header shows instead of the live map, for what the embed can't show or something shows better; a `Hero` or `Lead` that returns null leaves the header as it was), and a line in that folder's registry. `nearbySummary` makes the page's "Other places nearby" cards give each place's own summary of the topic and its distance (cameras, plate readers, transit, flights), and `overviewFirst` puts the topic's `Overview` above the overview's list of pages.
4. No other file changes: the route, sitemap, nav and cards pick the topic up from the registries.
5. Optionally, `places: '<topic id>'` on the layer pages it is the close-up of (`lib/platform/site/catalog/`): those pages then list the biggest places' pages of it (`components/site/places/topic-places.tsx`). The links go the other way through `parts/notes.tsx`, and `/city` and a country's place list link each place's topics.

## Rules for the copy

- Every sentence true of the data, and specific to the place: operators by name, counts, sources, distances. The layer pages already say how each layer works; a place page links to them (`parts/notes.tsx`) rather than repeating their paragraphs, which would make thousands of near-identical pages.
- Measured, modelled, estimated and timetabled are said as such. Counts carry the day or time they were true for.
- A shared place name is told apart by its region or country ("London, Ontario"); the most populous place keeps the plain name and slug.

## Data

- `gazetteer.json` (about 6 MB): GeoNames cities15000 (CC BY 4.0) without city sections, historical and abandoned places; admin regions and countries from GeoNames; labels from Wikidata (CC0) by GeoNames id (P1566), stored only where they differ from the English. Rebuilding asks Wikidata one batch at a time and caches the answers in `.cache/site`; `--refresh-names` asks again.
- `towns.json` (about 7 MB, one town a line): GeoNames cities1000 (CC BY 4.0), every place of 1,000 people or more without the same sections, from `tools/site/build-towns.ts`. Not for pages: the map's status bar names the town under the pointer from it (`apps/api/src/v1/geo/nearest-place.ts`), so `geo/reverse` asks Nominatim only out of every town's reach. Without it the api falls back to `gazetteer.json`.
- `topics/transit.json`: from `public/data/transit/cities.json`, whose cities are GeoNames places, with each operator's feeds read for how their vehicles reach the map.
- `topics/flights.json` and `topics/weather.json`: NOAA's station list (airports are its ranks 0 to 3 with a TAF) and RainViewer's radar coverage tiles, sampled at each place. The weather page's forecast is MET Norway's Locationforecast (CC BY 4.0), fetched when the page renders through `/api/forecast` (`app/api/(feeds)/forecast`), which keeps each point, rounded to 0.01°, until MET's Expires, revalidates with If-Modified-Since and asks at most five times a second; the page shows the next day every three hours and seven days, in the place's time zone (from `topics/iss.json`) and its units (`copy/weather-words.ts`: the US imperial, the UK miles an hour). The airport reports read in the same units there (`describeEveryday`); the flights page keeps them as pilots write them.
- `topics/ports.json`: NGA's World Port Index (Pub. 150, public domain; cached in `.cache/site`) within 10 km of each place, each harbour credited to one place (one bearing its name, else the strongest pull of population over distance squared), with the map's major ports and chokepoints (`lib/feeds/maritime/ports.ts`) and the different ships heard near each in the 24 fullest hours of the last week of the AIS archive. Rebuilt without an archive it has no counts, and the 10-ship part of the bar drops out.
- `topics/hazards.json`, from `tools/site/topics/hazards.ts` (`--as-of` sets the day counted to): three pages a place, the last ten calendar years, the last hundred and the whole record. USGS ComCat (M4.5+ for ten years, M6+ since 1900, asked a year at a time and cached in `.cache/site/hazards`) and NOAA NCEI's significant quakes, tsunamis and eruptions from `data/db/history.db`, never summed: a NOAA quake within 120 s and 150 km of a USGS one is the same quake. A country counts an event inside it, at sea within 100 km (every country that near), or up to 300 km where it is the nearest; a city, within 100 km, or 200 km for M7+. A tsunami also counts where NCEI's runups record deaths or a metre of water, with the dead there (a country's "ALL OF" summary or the sum of its points, whichever is larger) rather than the event's whole toll. Each range keeps its counts, its yearly or ten-yearly USGS series and its strongest, deadliest, latest and oldest events; the copy is chosen from them in `topics/hazards-facts.ts`. About 1.5 MB.
- Right now (`topics/now.ts`, `/city/<place>/now` and `/country/<place>/now`) has no table of its own: it reads the weather, flights, cameras, transit and ports tables for what else is live at the place, and asks `/api/near-now` (the phone widget's feed, `lib/feeds/hazards/near-now.ts`) for the warnings, quakes, fires, lightning and rain around it when the page renders. Indexed in cities of 100,000 or more where radar or a lightning network reaches and three of those live pages exist: 1,446 of 31,762 cities on 2026-10-26. It is not on the overview's live box, which the weather topic's rain and lightning lines would repeat. A country's page counts what is in force or recent inside the box its hazards row frames it with (`/api/near-now?bbox=`), so it says "in and around" (just across a border counts too), names one of each kind, and links its eight biggest cities' own pages; lightning and rain, read around a point, stay with the cities. Indexed for countries of a million people (203 have a box).
- `topics/attractions.json` (`/city/<place>/things-to-do`, `/country/<place>/things-to-do`), from `tools/site/topics/attractions.ts`: museums, castles, palaces, cathedrals, zoos and aquariums, gardens, parks, squares, monuments, fountains, statues, tourist attractions and World Heritage sites (P1435) from Wikidata (CC0), read through QLever's public endpoint (qlever.dev, no key) and cached in `.cache/site/attractions`. Each needs coordinates and articles in three Wikipedias (five for squares, fountains and statues), and counts for one city only, the strongest pull of population over distance squared within 30 km, as the ports table credits harbours, so a district doesn't repeat its city's list. A city keeps its 40 best known, a country its 40 best known by P17. Names come in our ten languages where Wikidata has them, and each links the reader's Wikipedia through Wikidata's GoToLinkedPage, English where theirs has no article. No pictures (Commons images are licensed one by one) and no map layer yet: "On the map" only centres the map. Indexed where eight items have articles in five Wikipedias (ten for a country).
- Sunrise and sunset (`topics/sun.ts`) has no table of its own: it takes each city's time zone from `iss.json` and works out today, yesterday and the place's calendar year when the page renders, from NOAA's solar equations (`topics/sun-times.ts`, tested against published times to two minutes), kept per place and day. The index bar is applied at run time (`SUN_INDEX`).
- Prayer times (`topics/prayer.ts`, `/city/<place>/prayer-times`) has no table either: the same time zones, and today's and the next 30 days' Fajr, sunrise, Dhuhr, both Asr times, Maghrib and Isha worked out when the page renders with the `adhan` library (MIT), kept per place and day, with the Qibla's great-circle bearing and distance. The method is the country's, from the cited table in `topics/prayer-methods.ts` (Diyanet for Turkey, Umm al-Qura for Saudi Arabia, Karachi with the Hanafi Asr for South Asia, ISNA for the US and Canada, the Moonsighting Committee for the UK, JAKIM's 18° for Malaysia, the national ministries' angles for Morocco, Tunisia, Jordan and Russia, the Muslim World League elsewhere); Asr is worked out from the shadow at that day's noon (`shadowTime` in `topics/sun-times.ts`) and rounded up, since adhan's own put it up to three minutes before the published times; beyond 48° Fajr and Isha are held to a seventh of the night, and a day without sunrise or sunset takes the nearest latitude that has one (aqrab al-bilad), each said on the page where it applies. Hijri dates and Ramadan come from ICU's Umm al-Qura calendar (`topics/prayer-hijri.ts`). `topics/prayer-times.test.ts` checks seven cities against aladhan.com, diyanet.gov.tr and e-solat.gov.my to two minutes. It shares the sun's bar, in the cities `faith-reach.ts` gives it (below).
- Ramadan (`topics/ramadan.ts`, `/city/<place>/ramadan-<year>`, named for the Gregorian year it begins) is the same again for each day of a Ramadan: imsak, Fajr, iftar, Isha and the fast's length, with its start, Laylat al-Qadr and Eid al-Fitr from the Umm al-Qura calendar. `topics/ramadan-calendar.ts` has a page for every Ramadan from 1435 (2014) to nine after the next not yet begun (2035 today), worked out when asked, so the range moves on by itself. Only the one under way or next and the three after it are indexed and in the sitemap; the others render with `noindex`. A second Ramadan beginning in the same year (2030) adds its Hijri year to the slug. Imsak is Fajr except where a country's timetables put it 10 minutes earlier (Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Brunei; `imsakMargin`). `topics/ramadan.test.ts` checks 1448 against Diyanet's and JAKIM's published timetables and aladhan.com to two minutes.
- Shabbat times (`topics/shabbat.ts`, `/city/<place>/shabbat-times`): candle lighting and havdalah for this Shabbat and the next 12 weeks, with the holidays among them, worked out when the page renders (`topics/shabbat-times.ts`) as Hebcal does: candles 18 minutes before sunset, 20 in Israel, 40 in Jerusalem and 30 in Haifa and Zikhron Ya'akov (hebcal-web's `urlArgs.js`), the minute of sunset taken first; havdalah, and a holiday's second-night candles, when the Sun is 8.5° below the horizon, to the nearest minute. Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret, Pesach and Shavuot come from ICU's Hebrew calendar, with the second days outside Israel. Checked against hebcal.com for nine cities over 12 weeks and Pesach, Shavuot and Tishrei 2027: 463 of 464 times the same minute (`topics/shabbat-times.test.ts`).
- Faith pages (`faith: true` on the topic: prayer times, Ramadan, Shabbat and the Prayers page) follow `topics/faith-reach.ts`. Prayer times and Ramadan exist where the country's Muslim share is 10% or more, or 1% with 300,000 people, or in Xinjiang, Ningxia, Gansu, Qinghai, Thailand's four southern provinces and Bangsamoro (`muslim-share.json` from `tools/site/topics/muslim-share.ts`, Wikipedia's "Islam by country"); Shabbat times in Israel and the cities with 10,000 Jews or more (`jewish-communities.json` from `tools/site/topics/jewish-communities.ts`, Wikipedia's "Jewish population by city"). Inside that they index on the sunrise bar, Shabbat also anywhere in its table. A city's own pages link only its country's majority faith (`faithShown`: Muslim share 50% or more, Israel for Shabbat), through one nav entry, its Prayers page (`topics/prayers.ts`, `noindex`), which every faith page marks as current; the other faiths' pages are reached from search and the dates pages (`/ramadan`, `/jewish-holidays`, which lists `shabbatCities()`). Faith pages stay out of every list of a place's topics: the overview's cards and copy, nearby cards, place lists, and the count toward an indexed overview.
- Camera pages show the cameras themselves, since they are what people come for and place pages have no picture of the map. The header shows the latest stills of the on-air cameras nearest the centre (up to four, one per junction, `frames` in `topics/cameras.ts`), straight from each camera's owner as the map's tiles load them (`previewMedia`, `lib/feeds/cameras/preview.ts`), asked for again every minute while on screen; one that fails gives its place to the next (`topics/parts/camera-frames.tsx`). Where the nearest cameras send only video (NDOT, NYSDOT), one player plays them when the reader presses Play, and not before (`camera-player.tsx`, hls.js loaded then). Where there is neither, the header keeps the live map. "See them on the live map" opens at zoom 13, where the map draws cameras as pictures, on the nearest one it draws so (`tileAt`). A city's overview shows the same stills above its pages, and its plate-reader page links its cameras. Hosts that answer every still with one "no live feed" picture are kept out of the frames (`PLACEHOLDER_STILLS` in `topics/camera-catalogue.ts`); the map's liveness check should flag them instead.
- `topics/iss.json` and `topics/aurora.json`, both from `tools/site/topics/sky.ts`: every city's time zone from GeoNames, and for the aurora the corrected geomagnetic latitude of places from 50°, traced along IGRF-14's field (NOAA NCEI, cached in `.cache/site`; it matched aacgmv2 to 0.1°). The passes and the aurora chance are worked out when the page renders, from the satellites and aurora routes.

- `topics/alpr.json`, from `tools/site/topics/alpr.ts`, and `us-areas.json`, from `tools/site/build-us-areas.ts`, read `data/db`, so they are built on the Mac and committed: `npx tsx tools/import/fetch.ts us-counties us-places deflock`, `npm run import -- seed us-counties us-places deflock-alpr`, then both builders. The importers keep DeFlock's readers (makers and operators folded to one spelling, bearings parsed, each US reader's county and Census place found by point-in-polygon) and the Census 2024 county and place boundaries, gazetteers and estimates. A US city or town counts the readers inside its Census place (2,816 of 3,122 GeoNames cities matched one: the place around the city's point bearing its name, or one bearing it within 20 km); a county or state, those inside its boundary; any other city, the readers no limits claim that lie nearest it relative to its size (`alprKm`: 4 km for a town, 25 km for a metropolis), so New York's boroughs have no page of their own and their counties stand in. Places are keyed by Census FIPS above GeoNames' range (`900013121` is Fulton County, `804872392` West University Place), because GeoNames still lists Connecticut's old counties. The copy says "Flock cameras" only where Flock Safety made most of a place's readers. About 4 MB.
- `topics/indicators.json`, from `tools/site/topics/indicators.ts`, reads `data/db/history.db` and `catalog.db` read-only, so it too is built on the Mac and committed (`nice npx tsx tools/site/topics/indicators.ts`, under a second). For every country with an entity in `catalog.db` (its ISO2), each of the 39 measures in `topics/indicators-data.ts` (the table, its groups and order, shared with anything else that charts them): the yearly series as History holds it, rounded to four significant digits, with a null for a year the source has no value for, never carried forward; the projection (`<code>.proj`) and its published range (`.proj.lo`, `.proj.hi`, shown lower to higher) kept apart, the estimates stopping where the country's own projection starts, as History joins them (`lib/history/projection.ts`); an older run in the same unit before the main one (life expectancy from Our World in Data, population from HYDE and Gapminder, from 1800), or a long run in another unit charted on its own (Maddison's GDP per person from 1820, with its citation wherever it shows); and the facts the copy uses: first, latest, highest and lowest, the projection's end, the place among countries with a value in the measure's rank year (the latest in which half of them have one; not said where the place is shared, as for most countries' zero conflict deaths), and the World's value. `meta` holds each measure's World series for the comparison line (left out where the World is all zeros, as for net migration), its source and licence, and its rank year. Military spending is SIPRI's own database (share of GDP, constant 2024 US dollars, per person, share of government spending), with SIPRI's estimates and highly uncertain figures kept from the points' flags and marked on the chart and in the table; armed forces are the IISS's through the World Bank, with the Correlates of War's military personnel before them back to 1816, and the Correlates of War's capability index is its own measure. A historical state's rows (the USSR, Prussia) are never drawn on a modern country: each line is the entity's own. Pages read it through `topics/indicators-table.ts` (`indicatorSeries(slug, GeoNames id or ISO2)`), as do the comparisons and world pages (`compare-pages.md`). Sources whose terms restrict commercial use are marked in the table (IMF's World Economic Outlook, SIPRI, IHME's Global Burden of Disease). About 7.7 MB.
- Public holidays (`topics/holidays.ts`, `/country/<place>/public-holidays-<year>`) are worked out when the page renders from the `date-holidays` rules (ISC, data CC BY 3.0; `topics/holidays-rules.ts`), for any year from ten before the clock's to ten after: this year and the next two are indexed, the rest render unindexed and unlisted, and the nav lists next year's page from October. `topics/holidays.json` (`tools/site/topics/holidays.ts`, about 6 KB, rebuilt each year) says which countries to trust and how: a country stands where its rules match an official list (GOV.UK's JSON and the Cabinet Office's CSV, read by the builder; OPM, Service-Public, Diyanet with Kanun 2429's half days, Mexico's labour law and Sweden's holiday act, checked by hand on 2026-10-21) or Nager.Date's weekday days off for the build year and the two after, and its regional holidays are listed only where their regions match Nager's too. GOV.UK's corrections are stored per year (Scotland's 2026 World Cup holiday); China's days off and make-up working days come from the State Council's yearly notice, entered in `topics/holidays-china.ts`, and a year without one shows the statutory days, unindexed. Left out: days off moved or set by yearly decree (Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the UAE), Ukraine's suspended holidays, Australia's state-set holidays, and every country the two sources disagree on (Canada, Switzerland, Brazil, South Korea, India and others): 47 countries on 2026-10-21. The bar is six days of national holidays in each checked year (`HOLIDAY_INDEX`); all 47 pass. Long weekends, bridge days and counts use the country's own weekend (CLDR) and only what applies everywhere, and a holiday shared with another (19 May, Kurban Bayramı 2027) counts once (`topics/holidays-facts.ts`).

## Measuring

Every public page (the layer pages, places, guides, About) counts a view as it opens: `components/site/page-view.tsx`, mounted in `SiteFrame`, sends `POST /api/v1/page-view` with the English path, the language, the outside site that sent the reader and the browser's visitor id. `apps/api/src/analytics/page-reads.ts` keeps one `page_read` row per browser, day, page, language and referrer (views capped at 50 a day), spends no map time, and is always open to the key policy. Rows go after 90 days with the other usage (`usage-prune.ts`) and with an account's linked browsers (`privacy/erase.ts`, `export.ts`). Admin → Analytics → Visitors keeps the daily counts (`analytics/pages.ts`) for its languages, sections and map time. Each view is also logged on its own in `page_view` (`analytics/page-reads.ts`): when, the page this tab showed before (`takip.page` in sessionStorage), the linking page on the referring site when that site's referrer policy shares it (kept for social and other sites only, never a search's or an AI chat's; `analytics/sources.ts`), the campaign tag on the address, browser, OS and device class, and the town, country and town position the address points to (the address isn't kept), at most 300 a browser a day, a reload within 10 s once. The map logs its own opening there as path `/` (`use-map-time.ts`). A page's small live map (the embed in `LiveWindow`) books each minute the reader moves, zooms, clicks or points at it (a mouse that stays a moment), and each minute a camera or channel plays in it on screen, by page and UTC hour, in `page_map_minute` (`embed/hooks/use-page-map-time.ts` → the same endpoint with `use: 'map'`), only when our own page frames it. Each such minute also keeps the reader on Live as using its small map, and a page shown again from the back-forward cache notes itself in `takip.page` again, so the minutes don't go to the page before. The Visitors tab shows those minutes beside the full map's (`analytics/map-minutes.ts`, from the hourly tables). `analytics/page-visits.ts` groups the log into visits (half an hour apart, or a fresh arrival from another site), and everything else on the Visitors tab is built from them (`analytics/journeys.ts`), so it keeps to a window of hours (`?hours=6|24|72`) exactly: visitors split into page readers and map users, per hour or day, the flow chart (source → landing page → map, another page or left), referrers grouped into sources and channels (search engines, AI assistants, social sites; `analytics/sources.ts`), per-page time before the next step and exits, page-to-page steps, weekday × hour, countries, cities and a map, new and returning browsers, devices, browsers and OSes, a page's own detail (`GET /api/admin/analytics/pages/page?path=`) and the recent-visits list (`GET /api/admin/analytics/pages/visits`, narrowed by source, page, country, channel, next step or new/returning). The server takes any lowercase path up to three deep except the app's own areas, so a new section counts without a change there. A reader's first page is also kept in the browser (`takip.arrival`, `lib/platform/visitor.ts`), so when they open the map their visitor row records that page and the site before it as how they arrived.
